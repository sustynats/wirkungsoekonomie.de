#### Problem / goal
A new procurement/tariff-compliance regime can create intended labour-standard effects and unintended procurement/compliance effects. Monitoring effectiveness **and** practicality is therefore a distinct governance lever that can reveal both sides; monitoring alone does not change the law.

#### A→M→ΔZ→R
`A`: continuously observe effectiveness and practicability of the existing TVgG/VgMinArbV framework  
`M`: collect/review evidence on labour-standard delivery, procurement participation, compliance/enforcement and administrative burden  
`ΔZ`: decision-makers can identify underperformance, burden, evasion or distributional effects and have a basis for revision  
`R`: potential improvement in regulatory quality, fair-work protection and public-procurement functioning **only if** findings are methodologically sound, transparent and connected to actual correction/revision decisions.

#### Competence/system boundary
`LAND_MV_PROCUREMENT_LAW_MONITORING_AND_REGULATORY_REVISION_WITH_MUNICIPAL_PROCUREMENT_ENFORCEMENT_TARIFF_PARTY_AND_HIGHER_LAW_DEPENDENCIES`.

#### Required monitoring dimensions / omissions
The programme does not specify:
- indicators or evaluation protocol;
- publication/independence;
- baseline/counterfactual;
- review cadence despite `laufend`;
- threshold for revision;
- data on bidders, SMEs, award prices/time, exclusions, complaints, wages/conditions, compliance checks or sanctions;
- differentiation between legal design, implementation and market conditions.

These are open Delivery/Evidence fields, not a reason to deny that monitoring itself is an identifiable action.

#### Trade-offs / distribution
A stricter or administratively complex regime can affect workers, SMEs/large firms, procurement bodies, municipalities and taxpayers differently. Monitoring must therefore not collapse `more tariff compliance` and `procurement practicality` into one compensating score.

#### Noncompensation
Lower administrative burden cannot compensate for unlawful/unsafe/substandard employment conditions; conversely, formal compliance counts cannot compensate for a procurement system that materially excludes capable SMEs or fails to deliver needed public services. Both hard dimensions must remain visible.

#### Reality / falsification
Track at least:
- tender/bidder participation, especially SME and regional distribution where lawful;
- procurement duration/transaction cost/complaints;
- tariff/minimum-working-condition compliance and enforcement findings;
- contract price/value and service delivery;
- evasion/subcontracting effects;
- worker outcomes where attributable;
- publication of findings and concrete rule/process revisions tied to evidence.

The positive adaptive-governance assessment is weakened if `monitoring` produces no defined evidence, no transparent review, or no revision path despite identified defects.
