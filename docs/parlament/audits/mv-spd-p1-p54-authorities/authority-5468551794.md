## 4. `MV-SPD-2026-SU-00047-A03` — introduce a `qualifizierte Volksbefragung`

Exact action: `Um Bürgerinnen und Bürger stärker an Entscheidungen zu beteiligen, wollen wir die qualifizierte Volksbefragung einführen.`  
Atom SHA-256: `0d1bc916635ea2402f711af2305fbb387c8d796178c3d00a1f27086cdd6ec787`

`terminal_fach_state = EXPLICIT_FACH_APPROVED`

`impact_direction = AMBIVALENT_DIRECT_PARTICIPATION_AND_PUBLIC_PREFERENCE_SIGNAL_POTENTIAL / CONSTITUTIONAL_DESIGN_REPRESENTATIVENESS_AND_DECISION_LINK_DEPENDENT`

`evidence_level = MEDIUM_FOR_PARTICIPATION_AND_PREFERENCE_SIGNAL_MECHANISM / LOW_FOR_DECISION_QUALITY_TRUST_AND_POLICY_OUTCOME`

### Problem / goal
The object addresses the distance between representative decision-making and direct citizen input by proposing an additional statewide participation instrument. Existing M-V law already contains Volksinitiative, Volksbegehren and Volksentscheid; therefore the relevant counterfactual is **not** `no participation`, but the current representative-plus-plebiscitary architecture plus non-binding participation processes.

### Legal / competence boundary
`LAND_MV_CONSTITUTIONAL_AND_STATUTORY_DESIGN / TWO_THIRDS_CONSTITUTIONAL_CHANGE_LIKELY_REQUIRED_FOR_LEGISLATIVE_SUBJECT_VOLKSBEFRAGUNG`

The current Art. 59/60 architecture does not itself provide a general Volksbefragung. The Landtag’s prior legal analysis explicitly states that a Volksbefragung on legislative questions/pre-questions requires a constitutional amendment. Thus this is not a simple executive-programme implementation. The exact 2026 proposal also omits whether the instrument would be binding/advisory, who can trigger it, what topics are excluded and how it relates to Parliament.

### A→M→ΔZ→R
`A`: create a constitutionally/statutorily valid statewide qualified consultation instrument  
`M`: eligible citizens can cast an additional structured vote/opinion signal on specified public questions  
`ΔZ`: decision-makers receive a formally generated preference signal and citizens obtain an additional participation channel  
`R`: possible democratic participation/legitimacy/information gains **or** distortions depending on turnout, framing, eligibility, campaign conditions, subject matter and the rule connecting the result to later decisions.

### Material omissions / trade-offs
- trigger actor and threshold;
- advisory versus binding legal consequence;
- permissible/excluded subjects, especially budget/tax/fundamental-rights/minority-rights matters;
- voter eligibility and quorum;
- question-drafting neutrality and review;
- timing relative to elections/parliamentary procedure;
- campaign finance/information rules;
- accessibility, language, disability and non-digital access;
- misinformation/agenda-setting and mobilisation asymmetry;
- cost and administrative capacity;
- constitutional review and dispute route;
- response obligation when government/parliament departs from result.

Potential gain: additional formal participation and transparency about preferences.  
Potential risk: turnout/participation bias, binary simplification of multi-dimensional problems, agenda capture, polarisation, ambiguity over the result’s legal/political weight, or displacement of deliberative/parliamentary responsibility.

### Distribution / noncompensation
Participation must be disaggregated by age, region, education/income proxies where legally/ethically possible, disability/accessibility and other participation barriers. A high aggregate turnout cannot compensate for an unconstitutional subject, discriminatory exclusion, misleading question design or violations of fundamental/minority rights. Nor can a plebiscitary result by itself replace evidence, rights review or parliamentary responsibility for complex trade-offs.

### Time horizon
`MEDIUM_TO_LONG`: constitutional design/adoption first; only then operational consultations and subsequent democratic-outcome rechecks.

### Reality / falsification
Track at minimum:
- legal adoption and constitutional validity;
- trigger/topic/question-design rules;
- turnout and representativeness/distribution;
- accessibility and invalid-ballot rates;
- information/campaign conditions;
- administrative cost/time;
- how results change, inform or fail to change decisions;
- trust/legitimacy only through independent before/after or comparative evidence, not self-reporting by proponents;
- litigation/rights conflicts and whether minority/fundamental-right safeguards hold.

The positive side of the ex-ante judgement is weakened if the instrument produces mainly symbolic consultation, systematically skewed participation, legally ambiguous mandates or lower-quality/rights-incompatible decisions.
