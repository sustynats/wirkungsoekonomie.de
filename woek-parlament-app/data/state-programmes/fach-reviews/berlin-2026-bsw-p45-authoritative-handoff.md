### Current legal/system baseline

- The EU formally adopted Regulation (EU) 2026/261 on 26.01.2026 to phase out Russian pipeline gas and LNG. The stepwise prohibition started applying in March 2026, with transition periods and full LNG/pipeline prohibitions scheduled across 2027. This is a directly applicable EU/federal energy-trade framework, not a Berlin import switch. Council: `https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/01/26/russian-gas-imports-council-gives-final-greenlight-to-a-stepwise-ban/` and timeline `https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/ending-russian-energy-imports/timeline-ending-russian-energy-imports/`.
- Existing German energy law already restricts disconnections for non-payment. Since the 2025 reforms, §§41f/41g EnWG apply proportionality, notice and protection for vulnerable households; Grundversorgung also provides an Abwendungsvereinbarung route. BNetzA: `https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Vportal/Energie/RechnungenSperrungen/start.html`.
- Energy prices depend on wholesale commodity prices, contracts, taxes/levies, grid costs, capacity/infrastructure, demand, risk premia, storage, weather and policy. A one-cause narrative is not accepted without decomposition.

- Mechanism: preventing affordability/non-payment-related disconnection protects heating/electricity access and can reduce health, housing and crisis harms; unpaid liabilities do not disappear and must be allocated/financed.
- Scope guard: the literal phrase `aus irgendwelchen Gründen` cannot safely be read to prohibit technical/safety/emergency outages or lawful grid-protection actions. Fach approval attaches to the social-disconnection policy lever; operational system-safety interruptions remain a separate domain.
- Competence: `MIXED_FEDERAL_ENERGY_CONTRACT_LAW / LAND_SOCIAL_SUPPORT_AND_PUBLIC_UTILITY_INFLUENCE`; Berlin cannot simply rewrite the entire national supply-contract regime.
- Risks/trade-offs: arrears accumulation, higher socialised tariffs/taxes, utility liquidity, strategic nonpayment, cross-subsidy distribution, delayed debt counselling and blurred responsibility between supplier/social state.
- Noncompensation: collection efficiency cannot justify life/health-dangerous disconnection; conversely universal service access does not erase the need for sustainable debt/funding design.
- Reality check: disconnections avoided, arrears/debt trajectory, household health/housing crises, repayment/support take-up, tariff/fiscal incidence, supplier bad debt and vulnerable-household reach.
